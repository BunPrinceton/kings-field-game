# Narrative Design Document
## King's Field-Style Dungeon Crawler

---

## I. WORLD LORE & DUNGEON BACKSTORY

### The Sunken Archive

Long before the kingdoms of men rose to power, there existed the Order of the Eternal Vigil—scholars who sought to transcend mortality itself through forbidden knowledge. Their vast underground archive, carved deep beneath the earth, was both library and laboratory, temple and tomb.

They succeeded, in a way. They did not die. But what they became was no longer human.

The Archive fell silent three centuries ago. The entrance sealed itself. The surrounding villages whispered of lights beneath the earth, of voices calling from abandoned wells, of those who ventured too close and returned... changed. Empty. Worn.

The seals have begun to fail.

### Fragments of Truth

The Order did not worship gods—they sought to understand the fundamental architecture of existence. Their research focused on:

- **The Stillness Between Moments**: Time as a physical substance to be manipulated
- **The Weight of Memory**: Consciousness as pattern, transferable and eternal
- **The Hollowing**: The price of knowledge, the erosion of self

Their final experiment was meant to preserve their collective knowledge forever. Instead, it trapped them—consciousness without context, memory without meaning, existing in an eternal present where past and future have collapsed into an agonizing now.

The dungeon is not merely old. It is *stuck*. The same torches have burned for three hundred years. The same dust hangs in the same air. And the scholars walk their halls, performing the same rituals, unaware that their experiment succeeded too well.

### Why the Archive Calls

The seals are failing not from without, but from within. The trapped consciousnesses are degrading, fragmenting, seeking new vessels to pour themselves into. Fresh minds. Fresh memories. The Archive hungers for what it has lost.

But there is another reason to delve into the depths: the Order's research was real. Somewhere in the Archive lies the Codex of Undoing—a text that could reverse the experiment, free the trapped scholars, or grant its reader power over time itself.

Multiple parties seek it. Not all of them are human anymore.

## II. PLAYER CHARACTER

### The Seeker

You are nameless by choice. Your past is your own burden to carry.

What brought you to the Archive's threshold:
- **Revenge**: Someone you loved descended into these depths and never returned. You will find them, or find what remains.
- **Obsession**: You have dreamed of the Archive since childhood—visions of endless corridors, whispering voices, a book written in your own hand. You must know why.
- **Desperation**: You are dying. Slow, inevitable, irreversible. The Order conquered death. Perhaps their secrets can save you.
- **Duty**: You serve powers that fear what sleeps below. You are here to ensure certain knowledge stays buried.

Whatever your reason, you know three truths:
1. The Archive does not release those who enter
2. The deeper you descend, the more reality frays
3. You will change. The only question is whether you will recognize what you become.

### Player Starting Text

*You stand before the iron gate, its surface covered in script you cannot read but somehow understand: "To seek is to hollow. To know is to cease. Beyond this threshold, time has forgotten its purpose."*

*The gate opens at your touch, as if expecting you.*

*The air that escapes smells of dust and candle wax and something else—the absence of decades, compressed into a single, patient breath.*

*You descend.*

## III. ENEMY DESCRIPTIONS

### The Remnant (Current Basic Enemy - Red Sphere)

**Visual**: A crimson orb that hovers at chest height, its surface roiling like disturbed water. Occasionally, shapes press against it from within—a hand, a face, screaming silently.

**Lore**:
These are what remains when a scholar's consciousness fragments completely—raw emotion and reflex without thought or purpose. They are drawn to living minds like moths to flame, seeking to devour what they can no longer possess.

The red glow is not light but concentrated *want*—the desperate, animal need to exist, to feel, to remember what it was to be whole.

They do not die easily because they have already died once.

**Examine Text**:
*"A shuddering sphere of condensed anguish. You feel its hunger before you see it. It does not know what it wants from you. Only that it wants."*

**Defeated Text**:
*"The sphere collapses inward, and for a moment you hear something—a sigh, perhaps, or the memory of one. Then silence."*

---

### Future Enemy Types

#### The Scribe (Proposed Medium Enemy)

**Concept**: Hunched, robed figures that carry quills and parchment. They scratch constantly at scrolls, recording everything they observe. When they notice you, they write faster, and their words manifest as hostile sigils that float toward you.

**Lore**:
Low-ranking members of the Order, trapped mid-documentation. They cannot stop recording. They do not recognize that the same moment has been logged thousands of times. When they write about intruders, their observations become prescriptive—they write what *should* happen, and the Archive enforces their narrative.

**Attack Pattern**: Ranged sigil attacks, medium health, retreat behavior when approached.

**Examine Text**:
*"It writes: 'The intruder bleeds. The intruder falls. The intruder is catalogued.' You feel the weight of its certainty pressing against possibility."*

---

#### The Archivist (Proposed Heavy Enemy)

**Concept**: Tall, gaunt figure in pristine robes, carrying a heavy tome. Moves deliberately, purposefully. When it "reads" from its book, reality around it shifts—walls relocate, doors seal, gravity wavers.

**Lore**:
The senior scholars who directed the experiment. Their consciousness remains mostly intact, but they no longer distinguish between organizing knowledge and organizing reality. To them, everything is simply data to be categorized, including you.

**Attack Pattern**:
- Slow movement
- Powerful reality-warping attacks
- Can "reorganize" the environment (block paths, create barriers)
- High health, high damage
- Vulnerable during "reading" animation

**Examine Text**:
*"It has not looked up from its book in three centuries. The pages are blank. This does not concern it."*

---

#### The Echo (Proposed Environmental Hazard)

**Concept**: Not quite an enemy. Translucent, ghostly figures that walk fixed paths, repeating the same motions endlessly. Passing through them inflicts cold damage and temporary confusion.

**Lore**:
Memory loops, fragments of moments caught in temporal amber. They are not aware and cannot be killed—you can only avoid them or endure them.

**Examine Text**:
*"A scholar, walking to a lecture they gave three hundred years ago. They will never arrive. They will never stop trying."*

---

#### The Amalgam (Proposed Boss Enemy)

**Concept**: A horrific fusion of multiple Remnants that have merged together, creating a massive, churning mass of red energy with dozens of half-formed faces and grasping hands.

**Lore**:
When Remnants consume enough life force, they sometimes combine, creating something with fragments of intelligence—a terrible collective consciousness that remembers being human and hates you for still being whole.

**Examine Text**:
*"Seventeen voices scream in harmony. They remember their names. They cannot agree on which is real."*

## IV. ITEM & WEAPON DESCRIPTIONS

### Weapons

#### **Worn Shortsword** (Starting Weapon)
*"A practical blade, well-maintained but unremarkable. Its previous owner carved a single word into the pommel: 'RETURN'. They did not."*

- Damage: 25
- Range: Close
- Special: None

---

#### **Chronophage Dagger** (Rare Drop)
*"The blade seems to exist a fraction of a second ahead of where it appears. Strikes land before they are made. The Order used these to harvest moments from living subjects. The screaming is psychosomatic."*

- Damage: 20
- Range: Close
- Special: Attacks have a chance to interrupt enemy actions

---

#### **Null Mace** (Mid-tier Weapon)
*"Forged from compacted paradox—matter that both exists and doesn't. Heavy beyond its size. When it strikes, it doesn't damage so much as erase a portion of what was."*

- Damage: 40
- Range: Close
- Special: Slow attack speed, ignores armor

---

#### **Quill of the Final Theorem** (Legendary Weapon)
*"The Order's ultimate tool: a quill that writes reality. Each strike inscribes wounds into existence with scholarly precision. The ink never runs dry. It is not ink."*

- Damage: 35
- Range: Medium
- Special: Ranged attack, can mark enemies for increased damage

---

### Consumables

#### **Phial of Moments**
*"A crystallized droplet of time, stolen from somewhere that no longer exists. Drinking it grants the sensation of infinite duration—seconds stretch into hours. Use wisely. The debt comes due."*

- Effect: Slows enemy movement for 30 seconds
- Lore: The Order harvested these from test subjects

---

#### **Candle of Persistent Light**
*"This flame has burned since before the Archive fell. It will burn after you are gone. Holding it provides comfort, though you cannot explain why."*

- Effect: Increases visibility range, slight health regeneration over time
- Lore: Standard issue for junior scholars

---

#### **Fragment of Certainty**
*"A shard of concentrated belief, crystallized from a scholar who knew, absolutely, that they would succeed. They were wrong. The conviction remains."*

- Effect: Restores 50 HP
- Lore: Formed from the final thoughts of failed experiments

---

#### **Tincture of Forgetting**
*"Smells of lavender and mercy. The Order gave these to subjects after... difficult procedures. Some researchers took them recreationally. Some never stopped."*

- Effect: Removes negative status effects, minor HP restore
- Side effect: Screen briefly blurs

---

### Key Items

#### **Cipher Lens**
*"A monocle of warped glass. When worn, hidden text becomes visible, scratched into walls by those who learned too much. Most of it is warnings. None of it is helpful."*

- Effect: Reveals hidden messages and lore

---

#### **Waystone Fragment**
*"Warm to the touch, pulsing faintly. The Order used these to navigate the Archive's shifting geometry. This one is broken. It points somewhere that isn't on any map."*

- Effect: Fast travel unlock (future feature)

### Lore Fragments (Scattered Notes)

#### Fragment I: The First Experiment
*"Day 1: We successfully extracted Subject 12's memory of their seventh birthday. Subject reports feeling 'lighter.' We have stored the memory in crystalline matrix. It remains stable. Subject 12 no longer remembers having a seventh birthday. They seem... relieved?"*

---

#### Fragment II: Progress Report
*"Month 6: Seventeen subjects have volunteered for full consciousness transfer. The vessels are prepared. Council is concerned about ethical implications, but Project Lead assures us the subjects will thank us. I wonder if gratitude remains when you are no longer yourself."*

---

#### Fragment III: The Shift
*"Month 8: Something is wrong. The transferred consciousnesses are not stable. They are aware but not coherent. Subject 3 exists in seventeen different vessels simultaneously and is screaming in all of them. We cannot find the original. We are not sure there is an original anymore."*

---

#### Fragment IV: Final Warning
*"Day Unknown: Time has stopped meaning what it meant. I am writing this note for the fourth time today or the first time ever. Both feel true. Do not use the Codex. Do not attempt reversal. The experiment succeeded. That is the problem. We are preserved perfectly, eternally, and we cannot endure it. Seal the Archive. Let us fade. For mercy's sake—"*

*[The text ends mid-sentence]*

---

#### Fragment V: The Codex
*"The Codex of Undoing is not a book. It is the Archive itself—every wall, every stone, every torch is a page. To read it is to exist within it. To understand it is to become it. The only way to undo the experiment is to replace it. Someone must take our place."*

## VII. NPC CHARACTER CONCEPTS

### The Witness (Mysterious Guide)

**Location**: Appears at key locations, always watching from a distance

**Concept**: A figure in gray robes who is neither hostile nor helpful. They observe and occasionally speak, offering cryptic commentary. They claim to be recording your journey "for the Archive."

**Personality**: Detached, scholarly, occasionally shows flashes of concern or humor. Speaks in precise, academic language.

**Sample Dialogue**:
- *"Fascinating. You chose the left path. Subject 117 chose right. They survived eight minutes longer. I wonder if you will break the record."*
- *"Do you feel it yet? The weight of observation? Every step you take is being recorded. Every choice, catalogued. Does knowing this change your behavior? Please answer honestly. It affects the data."*
- *"You are still afraid. Good. Fear is rational. The last subject who conquered their fear walked directly into the Hollowing Chamber singing. We are still cleaning up."*

**Role**: Provides cryptic hints, lore exposition, and unsettling commentary. May be more than they appear.

---

### The Merchant of Lost Things (Vendor)

**Location**: Appears in safe rooms, sitting among scattered possessions

**Concept**: An elderly figure surrounded by objects left behind by previous delvers. Sells items and offers upgrades in exchange for "echoes" (currency/souls).

**Personality**: Weary, darkly humorous, has seen too much. Treats death as mundane. Shows rare kindness.

**Sample Dialogue**:
- *"Back again? You're persistent. Or foolish. The line blurs down here."*
- *"This blade belonged to someone who thought they were ready. You can have it. They won't be needing it. Neither will the person before them. Or the person before them."*
- *"Echoes, dearie. That's the currency here. Fragments of moments, crystallized want. The Archive generates them when someone dies reaching for something. There's no shortage."*
- *"Don't ask how I got here. Don't ask how I leave. Don't ask why I stay. Some questions are survivable only when left unasked."*

**Role**: Shop/upgrade system, occasional lore hints, provides sense of consistency and safety in changing environment.

---

### The Hollow Scholar (Tragic Figure)

**Location**: Encountered in the mid-game, non-hostile

**Concept**: A scholar who almost completed the ritual but retained partial self-awareness. Can speak but struggles to maintain coherent thought. Offers quests involving memory recovery.

**Personality**: Lucid moments alternating with confusion, desperate to remember who they were, helpful but unreliable.

**Sample Dialogue**:
- *"I... I was... my name is... was it Marcus? Martha? It begins with... no, that's someone else's name. I'm wearing their memory. Please, do you see a journal? Green cover? Or was it red? It contained my... my..."*
- *"[Suddenly clear] Listen. When you reach the Chronology Laboratory, do NOT touch the singing crystals. I don't remember why, but I remember the screaming that came after."*
- *"Sometimes I remember being seventeen people. Sometimes I remember being nobody. I think I prefer nobody. It's simpler."*

**Role**: Quest giver (fetch quests for memory items), provides warnings, represents what awaits the player if they fail.

---

### The Archivist Prime (Antagonist/Final Boss)

**Concept**: The head of the Order, fully aware of what happened but convinced it was necessary. Maintains the Archive's systems and views all intruders as threats to preservation.

**Personality**: Coldly logical, believes emotion is weakness, treats the trapped scholars as "acceptable losses for immortal knowledge."

**Sample Dialogue** (encountered briefly before final battle):
- *"You have progressed admirably. Your resilience is noted. However, you cannot be permitted to reach the Codex. The Archive must remain intact."*
- *"Suffering? A temporary state, soon to be irrelevant. We are beyond such mortal concerns. In time, even the screaming becomes data."*
- *"If you destroy this place, you destroy all we have preserved. Three hundred years of knowledge, gone. Is your freedom worth that cost? Think carefully, intruder."*

**Role**: Represents the Archives' will to preserve itself, final boss encounter, moral complexity (is destroying knowledge ever justified?).

## VIII. MERCHANT & QUEST-GIVER CONCEPTS

### Merchant System Concept

**Thematic Implementation**: Rather than traditional shops, vendors appear as "Memory Markets"—safe zones where possessions of the dead manifest. The Merchant curates these items.

**Currency: Echoes**
- Dropped by enemies (Remnants specifically)
- Found in environment (crystallized moments of desire)
- Gained by completing quests
- Lost on death but can be recovered from death location (Souls-like system)

**Vendor Types**:

1. **The Merchant of Lost Things** (Main Vendor)
   - Sells weapons, consumables, upgrades
   - Accepts echoes
   - Always has random inventory based on what delvers carried

2. **The Memory Keeper** (Upgrade Specialist)
   - Upgrades weapons using "Fragments of Identity"
   - Each upgrade requires rare materials + echoes
   - Weapons gain lore as they upgrade

3. **The Cartographer of Impossibilities** (Utility)
   - Sells map fragments
   - Offers fast travel unlocks
   - Provides hints about secret areas (for a price)

---

### Quest Concepts

#### Quest: "What Remains"
**Giver**: The Hollow Scholar

**Objective**: Recover three memory fragments scattered across the Archive

**Reward**: Access to memory-viewing system (reveals lore), unique consumable

**Narrative**: The Scholar believes recovering their memories might restore their identity. The fragments reveal they were complicit in the experiment and chose to forget. Player decides whether to give them the truth.

---

#### Quest: "The Unfinished Theorem"
**Giver**: The Witness

**Objective**: Find a specific book in the Library of Singular Moment, return without reading it

**Reward**: Powerful weapon/spell

**Twist**: The book whispers to you constantly, offering power if you read it. Reading it grants immediate power but locks you out of the "true" ending.

---

#### Quest: "Mercy for the Fallen"
**Giver**: The Merchant of Lost Things

**Objective**: Defeat five specific Remnants that were once colleagues of the Merchant

**Reward**: Special blessing (passive buff) and lore about the Merchant's past

**Narrative**: These specific Remnants have degraded beyond recognition. Destroying them is an act of mercy.

---

#### Quest: "The Observer's Record"
**Giver**: Environmental (found notes)

**Objective**: Find and activate three observation terminals to unlock secret area

**Reward**: Access to Observer's Sanctum, unique armor set

**Lore**: The Observer's Sanctum contains evidence that the experiment was sabotaged from within. Someone wanted it to fail. Who?

---

#### Quest: "Candles in the Dark"
**Giver**: Environmental (automatic on finding first Eternal Candle)

**Objective**: Light all twelve Eternal Candles throughout the Archive

**Reward**: Permanent health increase, achievement/trophy

**Narrative**: Each candle has a small inscription from scholars who lit them before the fall. They form a poem about hope.

## X. OVERALL TONE & WRITING STYLE GUIDE

### Core Principles

1. **Show Restraint**: Not every line needs to be horrifying. The mundane makes the horror sharper.

2. **Imply, Don't Explain**: Trust players to piece together lore. Fragments are more powerful than exposition.

3. **Respect Intelligence**: Players can handle ambiguity, moral complexity, and unanswered questions.

4. **Consistency in Voice**: The Archive has a personality—bureaucratic, precise, detached. All text should reflect this.

5. **Loneliness Is Horror**: Emphasize isolation. You are alone with your thoughts and the Archive's memories.

### Writing Style

- **Sentence Structure**: Mix short, punchy statements with longer, flowing descriptions. Create rhythm.
  - *"The door opens. Beyond, darkness. You hear breathing—slow, wet, patient."*

- **Present Tense for Immediacy**: Use present tense for examine text and immediate events.
  - *"The blood is fresh. It has been fresh for decades."*

- **Precision Over Drama**: Academic horror. Clinical descriptions of impossible things.
  - *"Subject's consciousness has dispersed across seventeen vessels. Integration unsuccessful. Termination impossible."*

- **Contradictions**: Embrace impossible statements that are both true.
  - *"The corridor extends forever. You can see the end."*

- **Second Person for Intimacy**: Pull the player into the narrative.
  - *"You feel it watching. You have always felt it watching."*

### Atmosphere Keywords

- Dust, stillness, precision, observation, weight
- Hollow, fragment, echo, remnant, persist
- Catalogue, record, preserve, contain, seal
- Erode, fray, scatter, dissolve, collapse
- Patient, eternal, endless, perpetual, frozen

### What to Avoid

- Modern slang or references
- Overwrought purple prose
- Jump scares in text form (cheap horror)
- Explaining the mystery completely
- Happy endings (bittersweet is acceptable)
- Clean victories (everything has a cost)

## XI. MUSICAL/AUDIO TONE REFERENCES

Though this is a narrative document, audio design supports the atmosphere:

- **Silence** is a tool. Long stretches of quiet footsteps and distant echoes.
- **Ambience**: Low drones, distant whispers just below comprehension, paper rustling, pen scratching.
- **Enemy Sounds**: Not roars but whispers, sighs, the sound of wet pages turning.
- **Music**: Sparse. When present, use string instruments played incorrectly, reversed piano, atonal choirs.
- **Safe Areas**: Almost too quiet. A single, distant music box melody. Unsettling in its normalcy.
